Recording Cloth Micro-Movements for Stealth Games

Capturing subtle cloth movements is critical in stealth game development, where player audio cues directly impact tension and AI detection mechanics. This article details the end-to-end technical pipeline used by sound designers to record, clean, process, and implement micro-movement Foley for clothing. It covers prop selection, ultra-low-noise microphone setups, surgical spectral editing, dynamic processing chains, and middleware implementation designed to make cloth sounds both hyper-detailed and gameplay-reactive.

Sourcing and Preparing Fabrics

Micro-movement Foley begins with material selection tailored to character outfits and game context:

  • Synthetic Fabrics: Ripstop nylon, Gore-Tex, and polyester blends yield crisp, high-frequency transients ideal for modern tactical gear, windbreakers, or parkas.
  • Organic Heavy Fabrics: Heavy canvas, denim, and twill produce low-frequency friction and soft scuffs suitable for military fatigues or workwear.
  • Organic Light Fabrics: Silk, linen, and aged cotton provide ultra-quiet rustles that work well for silent crouching or creeping movements.
  • Leather and Suede: Thick leather creates creaks and low-end thuds, while suede offers soft, continuous surface friction.

Foley artists often wash fabrics with or without fabric softeners to adjust stiffness. Removing tags, buttons, and metal zippers prevents unwanted transient spikes during micro-articulation performances.

Recording Setup and Microphone Techniques

Cloth micro-movements sit near the noise floor of standard audio equipment, requiring specialized hardware and environments.

  • Acoustic Space: A dead room or anechoic Foley stage with an ultra-low noise floor (ideally below 15 dBA) is mandatory to prevent room tone from masking delicate fabric rustles.
  • Microphone Selection: Small diaphragm condenser (SDC) microphones with low self-noise (under 10 dBA) and high sensitivity are the industry standard. Common choices include the Sennheiser MKH 8040 or MKH 50, and the Neumann KM 184. Ultra-sensitive contact microphones or binaural arrays are occasionally used to capture internal fabric tension.
  • Mic Placement: Place the microphone between 2 to 6 inches from the fabric. Using a shock mount and pop filter is essential to isolate mechanical vibrations and slight air bursts generated by fabric displacement.
  • Performance: The Foley artist articulates the fabric using fingertip rolls, slow-motion wrist twisting, and gentle surface rubbing rather than full-body movement. This isolates specific cloth physics—such as sleeve-to-torso drag or pant-leg friction—without breathing or shoe noise contaminating the take.

Processing and Restoration Pipeline

Raw micro-movement recordings require substantial cleanup to make them usable at high gain levels within an interactive mix.

  1. De-Noising and Restoration: High-gain amplification reveals preamp hiss and ambient noise. Spectral repair tools (such as iZotope RX Spectral De-noise) remove constant background noise without dulling the high-frequency air of the fabric. Mouth de-clickers and de-hum modules eliminate accidental performer artifacts.
  2. Subtractive Equalization: Apply a steep high-pass filter between 80 Hz and 120 Hz to strip rumble and handling noise. Notch out harsh resonant frequencies typically found between 2 kHz and 4 kHz caused by synthetic fabric crinkling.
  3. Dynamic Control: Use multi-band compression or dynamic EQ to smooth out inconsistent movement spikes. Fast attack and moderate release times maintain natural textures while keeping overall volume stable.
  4. Pitch and Texture Variation: Subtle pitch shifting (-2 to +2 semitones) and slight formant manipulation create multiple variations from a single recording session, preventing audible repetition in gameplay loops.

Middleware and Game Engine Implementation

For a stealth game, cloth audio must directly reflect character states and interact with the game's detection systems.

  • Locomotion Layering: In audio middleware like Wwise or FMOD, cloth movement is separated into distinct layers: a continuous low-level rustle tied directly to player movement speed, and discrete one-shot transients triggered by specific animation footplants, stance changes, or weapon aiming.
  • Parameter Curves: Game engines feed real-time velocity, stance (standing, crouching, crawling), and surface proximity into Real-Time Parameter Controls (RTPCs). Lower player speeds scale the volume down while applying a subtle low-pass filter to simulate smoother, quieter micro-movements.
  • AI Awareness Hook: The perceived loudness of the cloth layer is mirrored by an in-game telemetry parameter. If a player pivots too quickly while crouched, the resulting cloth transient spike raises the player's acoustic signature, alerting nearby enemy AI.
