Audio Distance Attenuation for Stealth Footsteps

In stealth games, maintaining clear auditory feedback for low-amplitude actions like crouching or sneaking is vital for player situational awareness. This article explains how game developers calculate and customize distance attenuation curves, establish reference radii, apply dynamic equalization, and leverage priority-based mixing to ensure quiet footsteps remain audible at close proximity without vanishing unnaturally or overwhelming the overall audio mix.

The Inverse Square Law and Game Audio Limitations

In the physical world, sound intensity decreases according to the inverse-square law, dropping by approximately 6 decibels (dB) every time the distance between the listener and the source doubles:

\[\text{Attenuation (dB)} = 20 \log_{10}\left(\frac{d_0}{d}\right)\]

While physically accurate, strictly applying a logarithmic inverse-square formula to game audio fails for stealth mechanics. Stealth footsteps are recorded or synthesized at a low baseline amplitude. If a pure logarithmic drop-off starts immediately from the source point (\(d_0\)), the sound rapidly drops below the game’s perceptual noise floor within one or two virtual meters, rendering nearby enemy movements undetectable.

Establishing the Minimum Distance (Inner Radius)

To counteract rapid volume loss, developers establish a minimum distance threshold (\(d_{\min}\)), often called the inner radius or reference distance.

Between the sound source and \(d_{\min}\), no distance attenuation is applied; the sound plays at 100% of its base stealth volume. By setting \(d_{\min}\) to encompass the typical "tactical zone" (usually between 1 and 3 meters around an enemy or player), the subtle footstep sounds—such as fabric rustle, heel-strike, or surface shifting—retain their native loudness throughout the immediate vicinity.

Custom Piecewise and S-Curves

Past the inner radius, game engines (such as Unreal Engine and Unity) and audio middleware (such as Audiokinetic Wwise and FMOD) allow developers to override pure logarithmic decay in favor of custom curves:

  1. Logarithmic with Offset: Keeps the sound relatively flat near the source, then initiates a controlled drop-off once outside the immediate danger zone.
  2. S-Curves (Sigmoid Functions): An S-shaped attenuation curve creates a gentle roll-off just outside the inner radius, steepens in the mid-range (where the player no longer needs exact spatial precision), and flattens out smoothly before reaching zero volume (\(d_{\max}\)).
  3. Piecewise Linear Curves: Audio designers map specific volume nodes manually:
    • 0m to 2m: 0 dB attenuation (fully audible).
    • 2m to 5m: -3 dB (gentle drop, retaining critical spatial clues).
    • 5m to 12m: -12 dB to -24 dB (steep drop into the background).
    • 12m+: Complete silence to free up audio voices.

Frequency-Dependent Attenuation and Air Absorption

Audibility is not merely a matter of decibel levels; human hearing relies on frequency cues to detect motion. Low-amplitude sounds are primarily recognized by their high-frequency transients, such as the scuff of leather or crunch of dirt.

Developers use low-pass filters mapped to distance attenuation curves to simulate air absorption. At close proximity (0 to \(d_{\min}\)), the low-pass filter remains fully open (20 kHz cutoff), preserving the transient sharpness that makes quiet footsteps cut through background ambience. As distance increases, the cutoff frequency drops sharply, quickly stripping out the transient detail and making distant sounds feel diffused.

Dynamic Range Ducking and Focus Mixes

Even with a tailored attenuation curve, low-decibel footsteps can be masked by ambient environmental sounds like wind, rain, or distant gunfire. To resolve this, developers link attenuation systems with real-time dynamic sidechaining:

  • Listener-State Ducking: When the player enters a crouched or stealth state, the audio engine automatically lowers non-critical ambient busses by 2 dB to 4 dB, lowering the game's overall noise floor.
  • Proximity Ducking: When a stealth sound triggers within the inner radius, high-priority footstep events subtly duck competing low-priority ambient beds directly in the listener's immediate spatial channel.
