Preventing AI Companions from Blocking Players

In narrative game development, preventing AI companions from trapping players in corridors is critical for maintaining immersion and preventing player frustration. Designers use a combination of dynamic collision systems, spatial tagging, predictive yielding behaviors, and tethering mechanics to ensure companions support rather than hinder traversal. This article explores the primary technical and design solutions used in modern game development to keep narrow pathways clear.

Dynamic Collision and Soft Capsules

Rather than using rigid, immovable collision meshes, developers often implement dynamic collision boundaries for companions. When a companion is idle or wandering, their collision capsule is active. However, when the player moves directly toward the companion, the engine softens or shrinks the AI's collision capsule. In some implementations, collision is disabled entirely between the player and the companion, allowing the player to pass through the AI while an evasive animation plays to visually justify the movement.

Predictive Yielding and Intent Detection

Advanced companion AI continuously monitors the player's velocity, facing direction, and input vectors. If the system detects that the player is walking toward a space the companion currently occupies, the AI triggers an evasive or yielding routine. Instead of waiting for physical contact, the companion calculates an escape vector perpendicular to the player's path and moves out of the way preemptively, stepping aside to let the player pass.

Corridor Tagging and NavMesh Markup

Game environments frequently feature dedicated markup within navigation meshes (NavMeshes). In narrow hallways, doorways, and choke points, level designers apply specific volume tags that alter companion behavior:

  • No-Stop Zones: Companions are forbidden from idling or entering conversational idle states inside these marked volumes.
  • Single-File Rules: In tight spaces, the AI pathfinding restricts the companion from walking parallel to the player, forcing them to adopt a strict single-file formation.
  • Through-Pass Priority: If both the player and the companion are in a narrow corridor, the companion automatically yields navigation priority, retreating or sprinting forward to reach a wider opening.

Adaptive Tethering and Trailing Offsets

Companion positioning is typically governed by dynamic tethering systems that place the AI relative to the player’s camera and motion vector. In open spaces, companions might walk alongside the player to facilitate dialogue. When entering tighter indoor environments, the tethering system shifts the companion’s target anchor point to a trailing offset directly behind the player or outside the primary camera frustum. This prevents the companion from entering a doorway before the player does.

Player-Driven Displacement

If avoidance routines fail and physical contact occurs, games often employ soft physics displacement. By giving the player character a higher physics mass or priority override, simply walking forward gently shoves the companion out of the path without stopping player momentum. The companion plays an interrupted stumble or step-aside animation to maintain physical believability.

Off-Screen Relocation

When an AI companion becomes completely wedged or stuck in geometry within a tight space, the system can use camera-frustum checks to resolve the issue invisibly. As soon as the companion leaves the player's field of view, the game can silently teleport the companion to an open navigation node behind or ahead of the player, instantly clearing the corridor without visual pop-in.
