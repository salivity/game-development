Best Platforms for Sharing Game Development Logs

Sharing game development logs, or "devlogs," is crucial for building an audience, gathering early feedback, and documenting your game's creation process. This article explores the top platforms for publishing devlogs, comparing their unique features, audience reach, and formatting styles to help you choose the best home for your game's journey.

Itch.io

Itch.io is one of the most popular platforms for indie game developers. It allows you to create a project page for your game and attach a blog-style devlog directly to it.

  • Why it works: It reaches a highly targeted audience of indie game players and fellow creators. Readers can download your demos or purchase your game directly from the same page.
  • Best for: Playable builds accompanied by regular progress updates.

YouTube

Video devlogs have exploded in popularity, offering a highly engaging way to show—rather than just tell—how your game is being made.

  • Why it works: Visual medium is perfect for showcasing gameplay, art style, and programming milestones. YouTube's algorithm can push your content to a massive global audience if your videos are entertaining.
  • Best for: Developers comfortable with video editing who want to show visual progress and build a highly engaged community.

TIGSource Forums

The Indie Game Source (TIGSource) forums are a classic, long-running hub for indie game developers.

  • Why it works: This is a developer-centric community. The feedback you receive here is often highly technical, constructive, and coming from industry peers who understand the challenges of game design.
  • Best for: Deep-dive technical write-ups, industry networking, and receiving expert feedback.

Reddit

Reddit features several active communities dedicated to game development, such as r/gamedev, r/indiegaming, and r/Unity3D.

  • Why it works: You can share text posts, images, or short video clips. Highly upvoted posts can drive massive traffic to your steam page or website.
  • Best for: High-concept showcases, quick updates, and asking specific questions to the community.

Medium or Personal Blogs

If you prefer complete control over your formatting, branding, and content monetization, self-hosting a blog or using Medium is an excellent choice.

  • Why it works: No platform limitations on post length, code block formatting, or image hosting. It is ideal for SEO optimization to attract organic search traffic.
  • Best for: Long-form, highly detailed technical tutorials and post-mortems.

Discord

While not a traditional blogging platform, Discord is unmatched for real-time community engagement.

  • Why it works: You can create dedicated channels for devlogs, sneak peeks, and patch notes. It allows for direct, daily interaction with your most dedicated fans.
  • Best for: Fostering a tight-knit community and running closed beta tests.
