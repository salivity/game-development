Calculating Mobile Game UA ROI Across Channels

Calculating user acquisition (UA) return on investment (ROI) in mobile game development requires tracking acquisition costs against player revenue across multiple paid and organic channels. This article outlines the essential formulas, metrics, and attribution frameworks game publishers use to measure channel performance, analyze cohort lifetime value (LTV), and allocate marketing budgets effectively.

The Fundamental ROI and ROAS Formulas

Mobile publishers evaluate UA success primarily through Return on Ad Spend (ROAS) and Return on Investment (ROI):

  • ROAS (%): (Total Revenue Generated by Cohort / Total Ad Spend on Cohort) * 100
  • ROI (%): ((Total Revenue - Total Ad Spend) / Total Ad Spend) * 100

A campaign breaks even when ROAS reaches 100%. If a publisher spends $10,000 on a channel and the acquired users generate $14,000 in revenue over a given period, the ROAS is 140% and the ROI is 40%.

Step 1: Measuring Total Acquisition Costs

To determine spend per channel, publishers calculate the effective Cost Per Install (eCPI) and Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC):

  • eCPI: Total Spend on Channel / Total Attributed Installs
  • Costs Included: Ad spend, creative production costs, agency fees, and platform distribution fees.

Publishers track spend across individual channels such as ad networks (AppLovin, Unity Ads), social media platforms (Meta, TikTok), search networks (Google App Campaigns), and influencer campaigns.

Step 2: Measuring Monetization and Cohort LTV

Mobile game monetization generally comes from three streams: In-App Purchases (IAP), In-App Advertising (IAA), and Subscriptions. Publishers track these revenue streams using cohort analysis, grouping users by their install date and acquisition channel.

Because players generate revenue over months or years, publishers project and track LTV milestones over standardized time windows:

  • Day 0 (D0) ROAS: Measures immediate revenue to evaluate initial campaign quality.
  • Day 7 (D7) / Day 30 (D30) ROAS: Benchmark metrics to determine early payback velocity.
  • Day 180 / Day 360 ROAS: Long-term maturity points where games reach full profitability.

Step 3: Implementing Attribution and Cross-Channel Tracking

Accurately mapping revenue back to the originating channel relies on specialized attribution tools:

  • Mobile Measurement Partners (MMPs): Tools like AppsFlyer, Adjust, or Singular aggregate cost data from ad networks and match it with downstream in-game purchase and ad revenue events via SDK integration.
  • Privacy Frameworks: Publishers use Apple’s SKAdNetwork (SKAN) for iOS and Google's Privacy Sandbox for Android. These frameworks require conversion value schemas to model early user behavior and predict long-term revenue under privacy limitations.
  • Media Mix Modeling (MMM): Macro-level statistical modeling used alongside deterministic attribution to quantify the efficiency of channels with limited tracking visibility.

Step 4: Comparing Cross-Channel Performance

Channels deliver different player types and monetization profiles. Publishers compare performance using normalized metrics:

Channel Type Typical Strength Primary Metric Focus
Social (Meta, TikTok) High targeting precision, scale D7/D30 IAP ROAS
Search (Google App Campaigns) High user intent Retention and high LTV
Ad Networks (Unity, AppLovin) High volume of active gamers Fast IAA monetization
Influencers / Programmatic Brand awareness and viral reach Blended organic uplift

Step 5: Accounting for Organic Lift and K-Factor

Paid UA often boosts organic visibility by improving store ranking. Publishers calculate the organic multiplier (K-Factor) to determine the blended impact of their marketing spend:

  • Blended eCPI: Total Marketing Spend / (Paid Installs + Organic Installs)

When a paid campaign drives incremental organic downloads, the blended eCPI decreases, shortening the payback period and increasing total channel ROI.
