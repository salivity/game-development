How to Negotiate Game Revenue Share Tiers

Negotiating tiered revenue splits tied to commercial milestones is a critical process for game developers and publishers aiming to align incentives and manage financial risk. This article outlines how these tiered agreements function, the mechanics of commercial milestones like recoupment and revenue thresholds, the primary levers both parties use during negotiations, and the standard terms required to ensure fair, transparent execution.

Understanding Tiered Revenue Sharing

A tiered revenue share agreement adjusts the percentage split between the developer and publisher as the game hits specific financial or unit-sales benchmarks. Rather than a flat split across the game’s lifecycle, tiers allow publishers to mitigate early investment risk while rewarding developers with higher percentages as the game proves commercially successful.

The Recoupment Baseline

Negotiations typically start with the recoupment phase, which dictates how revenue is distributed until the publisher recovers its initial advance, development funding, and agreed-upon marketing spend.

  • Standard Recoupment Split: Publishers commonly negotiate for 100% of net revenue until full recoupment. Alternatively, developers may negotiate a split (e.g., 80% publisher / 20% developer) during recoupment to maintain operational cash flow.
  • Recoupable vs. Non-Recoupable Costs: Developers should clearly define what costs are recoupable. Direct development funding and external QA/localization are standard, while general publisher overhead should generally be non-recoupable.

Structuring Post-Recoupment Milestones

Once the initial investment is paid back, revenue splits shift according to predefined commercial milestones. These tiers are commonly structured based on either gross revenue, net revenue, or total units sold.

1. Cumulative Net Revenue Tiers

Tying tiers to cumulative net revenue ensures splits change only after specific cash amounts are collected.

  • Tier 1 (Recoupment to $1M): 60% Developer / 40% Publisher
  • Tier 2 ($1M to $5M): 70% Developer / 30% Publisher
  • Tier 3 (Above $5M): 80% Developer / 20% Publisher

2. Unit Sales Tiers

Units sold provide an objective metric independent of regional pricing variations or deep platform discounts, though they can distort actual dollar earnings during seasonal sales.

  • Tier 1 (0 – 100k units): Base post-recoupment split
  • Tier 2 (100k – 500k units): Increased developer share
  • Tier 3 (500k+ units): Maximum developer share

Key Negotiation Levers

Both developers and publishers bring specific points of leverage to the negotiating table to determine where thresholds are set and how splits escalate.

  • Funding Amount and Risk: A publisher funding 100% of development carries higher risk and will push for higher baseline cuts and higher thresholds before the developer’s share increases.
  • Intellectual Property (IP) Ownership: If the developer retains 100% of the IP, the publisher may demand a larger revenue tail. If the publisher takes an ownership stake in the IP, the developer has leverage to demand faster tier escalation.
  • Marketing Commitments: Developers often tie revenue tiers to minimum marketing spend commitments from the publisher to ensure the publisher actively drives the game toward higher milestones.
  • Track Record and Existing Community: Studios with proven commercial successes or large pre-existing wishlists negotiate lower recoupment percentages and faster paths to high-tier splits.

Essential Contractual Definitions

To prevent disputes, commercial milestone negotiations must strictly define the underlying financial mechanics:

  • Defining "Net Revenue": Contracts must explicitly list allowable deductions from gross revenue (such as platform fees like Steam's 30%, value-added taxes, chargebacks, and refunds). Uncapped marketing expenses or ambiguous publisher service fees should not be deducted from net revenue calculations.
  • Audit Rights: Developers must negotiate the right to independently audit sales data and accounting books annually or semi-annually to verify milestone achievement.
  • Reporting Cadence: Terms should mandate monthly or quarterly sales reporting alongside payouts, clearly showing current progress toward the next commercial tier.
